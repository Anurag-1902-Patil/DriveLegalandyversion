EU DIRECTIVE 2015/413/EU OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL
of 11 March 2015
facilitating cross-border exchange of information on road-safety-related
traffic offences
(recast)
Official Journal of the European Union L 68, 13.3.2015, pp. 9–25
[Reproduced for legal-awareness purposes under EU open-data policy.
The binding text is the one published in the OJ. This plain-text version
is provided for RAG ingestion and is not a substitute for the original.]

========================================================================
PREAMBLE — RECITALS (Selected)
========================================================================

(1) Action by the Union in the field of road safety aims to reduce the
    number of deaths, injuries and material damage. An important element of
    the Union's road safety policy is the consistent enforcement of sanctions
    for road traffic offences committed in the Union which significantly
    endanger road safety, irrespective of the Member State of registration
    of the vehicle.

(2) Despite the progress made, too many road fatalities still occur in the
    Union, and enforcement action needs to be strengthened.

(3) The lack of effective cross-border enforcement of sanctions for road
    traffic offences results in situations where non-resident drivers of
    vehicles registered in other Member States escape sanctions for such
    offences. In order to remedy this situation, a more effective cross-border
    information exchange between Member States should be established.

(4) This Directive establishes a procedure to facilitate the cross-border
    enforcement of sanctions for road-safety-related traffic offences. That
    procedure is based on the exchange of vehicle registration data (VRD),
    which enables the Member State in which the offence was committed
    (Member State of the offence) to identify the registered keeper of the
    vehicle used to commit the offence.

(5) In order to ensure consistency, this Directive covers the eight main
    road-safety-related offences: speeding; failing to use a seat belt;
    failing to stop at a red traffic light; drink-driving; driving under the
    influence of drugs; failing to wear a safety helmet; use of a forbidden
    lane; unlawful use of a mobile telephone or other communication devices
    while driving.

========================================================================
CHAPTER I — GENERAL PROVISIONS
========================================================================

Article 1 — Subject Matter
This Directive lays down rules to facilitate the exchange of information
between Member States with a view to enforcing sanctions where road-safety-
related traffic offences are committed with a vehicle registered in a Member
State other than the Member State where the offence took place.

Article 2 — Scope
This Directive applies to the following road-safety-related traffic offences:
(a) speeding;
(b) failing to use a seat belt;
(c) failing to stop at a red traffic light;
(d) drink-driving (driving under the influence of alcohol);
(e) driving under the influence of drugs;
(f) failing to wear a safety helmet (for motorcyclists/moped riders);
(g) use of a forbidden lane (e.g., bus lanes, emergency lanes);
(h) unlawful use of a mobile telephone or other communication device
    while driving.

Article 3 — Definitions
For the purposes of this Directive:
(1) "vehicle" means any motor vehicle, including motorcycles, normally
    intended for use on roads;
(2) "Member State of the offence" means the Member State where the offence
    was committed;
(3) "Member State of registration" means the Member State where the vehicle
    involved in the offence is registered;
(4) "national contact point" means the competent authority designated by
    each Member State to handle requests for VRD exchange;
(5) "vehicle registration data" (VRD) means the data set referred to in
    Annex I, which enables identification of the registered keeper or owner
    of a vehicle;
(6) "registered keeper" means the person in whose name the vehicle is
    registered, as recorded in the vehicle registration database.

========================================================================
CHAPTER II — EXCHANGE OF VEHICLE REGISTRATION DATA
========================================================================

Article 4 — Procedure for the Exchange of Information
1. For the investigation of the road-safety-related traffic offences listed
   in Article 2, Member States shall allow other Member States' national
   contact points access to national vehicle registration data, with the
   power to conduct automated searching thereof, using a full vehicle
   registration number.
2. Such searches shall be conducted in accordance with the procedures
   described in Council Decision 2008/616/JHA and its Annex, which implement
   the Prüm Convention data-sharing arrangements.
3. The data elements to be exchanged are specified in Annex I to this
   Directive and include:
   (a) data relating to the vehicle: registration number, make, model,
       vehicle category;
   (b) data relating to the registered keeper or owner: surname, first name,
       address, date of birth (for natural persons); name/title and registered
       address (for legal persons).

Article 5 — Follow-Up Procedure
1. The Member State of the offence shall use the data obtained pursuant to
   Article 4 to send an information letter to the registered keeper or owner
   of the vehicle.
2. The information letter shall include at least:
   (a) the nature of the road-safety-related traffic offence;
   (b) the place, date and time of the offence;
   (c) the title of the legislation infringed and the penalty applicable;
   (d) the time limit for payment of any financial penalty, if applicable;
   (e) information about the legal proceedings initiated, including any
       right to challenge;
   (f) the name, address and other contact details of the body responsible
       for the proceedings.
3. The information letter shall be written in the official language of the
   Member State of registration or, if several languages are official in that
   state, in one of those languages; or, where practicable, in the official
   language of the Member State of the offence.
4. The Member State of the offence shall send the information letter in
   accordance with its national law. Where it decides to initiate proceedings,
   it may conduct those proceedings in accordance with its national and Union
   law.

Article 6 — Data Protection
1. The data processed pursuant to this Directive shall be processed only for
   the purpose of facilitating the exchange of information on the road-safety-
   related traffic offences listed in Article 2.
2. The provisions of Directive 95/46/EC (now replaced by Regulation (EU)
   2016/679 — GDPR) and national data-protection legislation apply to the
   processing of personal data under this Directive.
3. Member States shall ensure that data subjects (the registered keepers)
   can exercise their rights of access, rectification, and erasure as provided
   by applicable data-protection law.

Article 7 — Designated National Contact Points
Each Member State shall designate a national contact point to receive and
process requests for VRD exchange. Member States shall communicate the
details of their national contact point to the Commission.

========================================================================
CHAPTER III — REPORTING
========================================================================

Article 8 — Reporting Obligations
Member States shall send a report to the Commission every two years on
the application of this Directive, covering:
(a) the number of automated searches conducted in its territory as Member
    State of the offence;
(b) the number of requests received as Member State of registration;
(c) the number of information letters sent to non-resident offenders;
(d) the types of offences for which information was exchanged.

Article 9 — Commission Review
The Commission shall submit a report to the European Parliament and the
Council on the application of this Directive, based on the reports from
Member States, no later than three years after transposition.

========================================================================
CHAPTER IV — FINAL PROVISIONS
========================================================================

Article 10 — Transposition
Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations, and
administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive by
6 May 2015 (for Member States already bound by Directive 2011/82/EU)
or by 6 May 2017 (for other Member States).

Article 11 — Entry into Force
This Directive entered into force on 13 March 2015 (date of publication
in the Official Journal of the European Union).

========================================================================
ANNEX I — VEHICLE REGISTRATION DATA ELEMENTS
========================================================================
The following data elements shall be made available for exchange:
A. Data relating to the vehicle:
   — Registration number
   — Vehicle make (brand)
   — Vehicle model (commercial type)
   — Vehicle category / class
   — Country of registration

B. Data relating to the keeper/owner:
   — Surname (family name)
   — First name(s)
   — Complete address of residence/registered office
   — Date of birth (natural persons)
   — Company name and registered office address (legal persons)

KEY PRACTICAL EFFECTS FOR DRIVERS IN THE EU
(Summary for DriveLegal users)
========================================================================

1. EIGHT COVERED OFFENCES: If you commit any of the following in another
   EU Member State, the host country can identify you through your home
   country's vehicle registration database and send you an enforcement notice:
   • Speeding
   • Not wearing a seat belt
   • Running a red light
   • Drink-driving (DUI)
   • Drug-impaired driving
   • Not wearing a motorcycle helmet
   • Driving in a forbidden lane (bus lane, emergency lane)
   • Using a handheld mobile phone while driving

2. CROSS-BORDER NOTICE: You will receive an information letter at your
   registered home address, in your home language where possible, detailing
   the offence, the fine, and how to pay or contest it.

3. NO IMMUNITY FOR TOURISTS: Non-resident drivers cannot evade fines simply
   because they are from another EU country. Fines are enforceable across
   borders under this Directive.

4. DATA PROTECTION: Your personal data may only be used for the specific
   offence investigation. It is protected by GDPR.

5. PAYMENT AND CONTESTING: You have the right to pay the fine, contest
   the offence in the Member State of the offence, or (in some cases) have
   the fine enforced in your home country. The information letter will specify
   the options.

6. COUNTRY-SPECIFIC FINE AMOUNTS: The fine amount is determined by the law
   of the Member State where the offence occurred — not by your home country.
   Examples (approximate, subject to change):
   • Speeding >30 km/h over limit in Germany: €280+ (Bußgeld) + 2 points
   • Drink-driving (BAC > 0.05) in France: €135 fixed penalty + licence suspension
   • No seat belt in Spain: €200 fixed penalty
   • Handheld phone in Netherlands: €380 fixed penalty
   • Running red light in Italy: €167–€665 + 6 licence points
   Always verify the current fine schedule with the official authority of the
   relevant Member State.

7. EU COUNTRIES COVERED (as of 2024):
   Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark,
   Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia,
   Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania,
   Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden.
   (Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein participate under EEA arrangements.)

EU TRAFFIC LAW OVERVIEW — MEMBER STATE SPEED LIMITS
(indicative — always verify current domestic law)
========================================================================

Germany: Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 100 km/h; Motorway — advisory 130 km/h
         (no general limit on Autobahn, but temporary limits posted).
France:  Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 80 km/h; Dual carriageway 110 km/h;
         Motorway 130 km/h.
Italy:   Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 90 km/h; Dual carriageway 110 km/h;
         Motorway 130 km/h.
Spain:   Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 90 km/h; Dual carriageway 100 km/h;
         Motorway 120 km/h.
Netherlands: Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 80–100 km/h; Motorway 100–130 km/h.
Poland:  Built-up 50 km/h (day) / 60 km/h (night); Rural 90 km/h;
         Expressway 120 km/h; Motorway 140 km/h.
Belgium: Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 90 km/h; Motorway 120 km/h.
Sweden:  Built-up 50 km/h; Rural 80 km/h; Dual carriageway/Motorway
         110–120 km/h.

EU BLOOD-ALCOHOL LIMITS (indicative — most Member States):
General drivers: 0.05 g/dl (50 mg/100 ml). Sweden, Poland: 0.02 g/dl.
Czech Republic, Hungary, Romania, Slovakia: 0.00 g/dl (zero tolerance).
Professional/commercial drivers: 0.02 g/dl across the EU.

EU SEAT BELT ENFORCEMENT:
Mandatory for all passengers in all seats. Driver responsible for children
under 14. Child restraint required for children under 135 cm (or per
domestic regulation).

EU MOBILE PHONE:
Handheld use prohibited in all Member States. Fine ranges from €100
(Estonia) to €400+ (Netherlands, Belgium). Additional licence points apply
in most countries.
