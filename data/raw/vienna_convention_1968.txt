VIENNA CONVENTION ON ROAD TRAFFIC (1968)
Concluded at Vienna on 8 November 1968
United Nations Treaty Series, Volume 1042
[Public domain. Reproduced for legal-awareness purposes.]

========================================================================
PREAMBLE
========================================================================
The Contracting Parties, desiring to facilitate international road
traffic and to increase road safety through the adoption of uniform
traffic rules, have agreed as follows:

========================================================================
CHAPTER I — GENERAL PROVISIONS
========================================================================

Article 1 — Definitions
For the purposes of this Convention, the following expressions shall
have the meanings hereby assigned to them:
(a) "domestic legislation" means the complete body of national or local
laws and regulations in force in the territory of a Contracting Party;
(b) "built-up area" means an area with entries and exits specially
signposted as such, or otherwise defined in domestic legislation;
(c) "road" means the entire width of any way or street open to public
motor traffic;
(d) "carriageway" means the part of a road normally used by vehicular
traffic; a road may have several carriageways;
(e) "lane" means any one of the longitudinal strips into which the
carriageway is divisible;
(f) "intersection" means any level crossing of roads, junction or
bifurcation, including the open areas formed by such crossings,
junctions or bifurcations;
(g) "level crossing" means any level intersection between a road and a
railway or tramway track with its own track formation;
(h) "motorway" means a road specially designed and built for motor
traffic, which does not serve properties bordering on it, which is
provided with separate carriageways for the two directions of traffic
separated by a dividing strip not intended for traffic or by another
means, which has no level crossing with any road, railway or tramway
track, or footpath, and which is specifically signposted as a
motorway;
(i) "vehicle" means any conveyance, including a power-driven vehicle,
travelling on a road;
(j) "power-driven vehicle" means any self-propelled road vehicle other
than those which run on rails; this term also covers mopeds;
(k) "motor vehicle" means any power-driven vehicle which is normally
used for carrying persons or goods by road or for drawing, on the
road, vehicles used for the carriage of persons or goods; the term
covers trolley buses, i.e., vehicles connected to an electric
conductor and not running on rails;
(l) "trailer" means any vehicle designed to be drawn by a power-driven
vehicle; the term covers semi-trailers;
(m) "semi-trailer" means any trailer designed to be coupled to a motor
vehicle in such a way that part of it rests on the motor vehicle and
a substantial part of its weight and of the weight of its load is
borne by the motor vehicle;
(n) "cycle" means any vehicle which has at least two wheels and is
propelled solely by the muscular energy of the persons on that
vehicle, in particular by means of pedals or hand-cranks;
(o) "moped" means any two-wheeled or three-wheeled vehicle which is
fitted with an internal combustion engine having a cylinder capacity
not exceeding 50 cc (3.05 cu.in.) and a maximum design speed not
exceeding 50 km (31 miles) per hour;
(p) "motorcycle" means any two-wheeled vehicle with or without a sidecar,
fitted with a power unit; mopeds are not included if domestic
legislation classifies them as cycles;
(q) "driver" means any person who drives a vehicle, including cycles,
or who guides cattle, singly or in herds, or flocks, or draught,
pack or saddle animals on a road;
(r) "permissible maximum mass" means the maximum mass of the laden
vehicle declared permissible by the competent authority of the
State in which the vehicle is registered;
(s) "direction of traffic" and "appropriate" (as applied to a side of
the road) means the right-hand side if, under domestic legislation,
the driver must pass the oncoming traffic on his left; and the
left-hand side if, under domestic legislation, the driver must pass
the oncoming traffic on his right;
(t) "priority" means the right to pass before another road-user.

Article 2 — Annexes to the Convention
The following Annexes are attached to this Convention and form an
integral part thereof:
  Annex 1: Exceptions from the obligation to admit motor vehicles and
           trailers in international traffic
  Annex 2: Registration number of motor vehicles and trailers in
           international traffic
  Annex 3: Distinguishing sign of motor vehicles and trailers in
           international traffic
  Annex 4: Identification marks of motor vehicles and trailers in
           international traffic
  Annex 5: Technical conditions concerning motor vehicles and trailers
  Annex 6: Domestic driving permit
  Annex 7: International Driving Permit

Article 3 — Obligations of Contracting Parties
1. (a) Contracting Parties shall take appropriate measures to ensure
   that the rules of road traffic in force in their territories conform,
   in substance, to the provisions of Chapter II of this Convention.
   (b) Contracting Parties shall also take appropriate measures to
   ensure that the rules of road traffic in force in their territories
   with respect to the technical conditions which motor vehicles and
   trailers must satisfy conform to the provisions of Annex 5 to this
   Convention.
   (c) Subject to the exceptions provided for in Annex 1, Contracting
   Parties shall be bound to admit to their territories in international
   traffic motor vehicles and trailers which fulfil the conditions laid
   down in Chapter III of this Convention and whose drivers fulfil the
   conditions laid down in Chapter IV.

Article 4 — Signs and Signals
Contracting Parties shall, unless they have already done so, take the
necessary measures to conform, within the time-limits specified therein,
to the provisions of the Protocol on Road Signs and Signals opened for
signature at Geneva on 19 September 1949, or to the Convention on Road
Signs and Signals opened for signature at Vienna on 8 November 1968.

========================================================================
CHAPTER II — RULES OF THE ROAD
========================================================================

Article 5 — Force of Signs and Signals
1. Road users shall comply with the instructions conveyed by road signs,
road markings and traffic light signals, even if those instructions
appear to conflict with other traffic rules.
2. Instructions conveyed by traffic light signals shall take precedence
over those conveyed by road signs giving priority.

Article 6 — Instructions given by Authorized Officials
1. Road users shall comply immediately with any instruction given by
officials duly authorized for that purpose by the competent authority,
in particular by police officers.
2. Road users shall also comply with instructions given by flagmen or
other persons authorized to control traffic at roadworks, level
crossings, airports, public events and similar circumstances.

Article 7 — General Rules
1. Road users shall avoid any behaviour likely to endanger or obstruct
traffic, to endanger persons, or to cause damage to public or private
property.
2. It is recommended that domestic legislation should provide that road
users shall not endanger or unnecessarily obstruct traffic.
3. Drivers shall be in a fit state to drive and shall have adequate
knowledge and skills; however, the physical and mental fitness to drive
and the level of knowledge and skills required shall be determined by
domestic legislation.
4. Drivers of power-driven vehicles shall have the skills required for
driving and shall have adequate knowledge of traffic rules. This
requirement does not prejudice the right of persons to learn to drive
in accordance with domestic legislation.

Article 8 — Driver of Vehicle
1. Every moving vehicle or combination of vehicles shall have a driver.
2. It is recommended that domestic legislation should provide that
pack, draught or saddle animals and livestock shall be driven by a
driver.
3. Every driver shall at all times be able to control his vehicle or to
guide his animals.
4. A driver of a motor vehicle shall at all times ensure that he can
see ahead, to the right and to the left sufficiently well for safety.
5. Every driver of a vehicle shall at all times ensure that his
vehicle or its load does not prevent him from seeing sufficiently well,
that it does not endanger road users or the public and does not damage
public or private property.

Article 9 — Flocks and Herds
It is recommended that domestic legislation should provide that livestock
shall as far as possible be kept to the side of the road and that if
their number exceeds a certain figure they shall be divided into groups
of limited size with sufficient space between groups to allow traffic
to pass.

Article 10 — Position on the Carriageway
1. The direction of traffic shall be the same throughout the territory
of each Contracting Party and on all roads in that territory.
2. On roads with two or more lanes in the direction of travel, drivers
shall not use the lane nearest to the edge of the carriageway
appropriate to the oncoming traffic except to the extent necessary to
pass parked vehicles or vehicles moving in the same direction as
themselves; this restriction shall not apply when it is needed to
prevent accidents.
3. When a driver is on a carriageway where the lanes are marked, he
shall ensure that the vehicle remains in the lane he has chosen and
shall only change lanes after having ascertained that he can do so
without risk to himself or to other road users and after giving the
prescribed signal.

Article 11 — Overtaking and Passing
1. Overtaking shall be on the side away from the direction of traffic.
Where lanes are marked on the carriageway, overtaking may be done on
the right if the driver has chosen a lane to the left of another lane
used by a vehicle he intends to overtake, or on the left if the driver
has chosen a lane to the right.
2. Before overtaking, every driver shall, without prejudice to the
provisions of Article 7, paragraph 1, and to those of Article 14, make
sure:
   (a) that no driver who is following him has begun to overtake him;
   (b) that the driver ahead of him in the same lane has not signalled
       his intention to overtake another vehicle;
   (c) that the gap between him and the oncoming traffic is sufficient
       for him to overtake without endangering oncoming traffic, taking
       into account his speed and the speed of the vehicles being overtaken
       and the road conditions.
3. In application of the provisions of paragraph 2 of this Article, it
is forbidden to overtake:
   (a) at or approaching intersections and at or approaching level
       crossings without gates or barriers;
   (b) at or approaching the crest of a hill and, where visibility is
       insufficient, on bends;
   (c) in any other places of restricted visibility.
4. The driver of an overtaken vehicle shall reduce his speed if
necessary to enable the overtaking vehicle to clear it, and shall not
accelerate while being overtaken.

Article 12 — Keeping to the Right (or to the Appropriate Side)
The driver of a vehicle meeting an oncoming vehicle shall move to the
appropriate side; if in so doing he runs the risk of moving onto the
shoulder or of leaving the carriageway, he may reduce his speed and if
necessary stop.

Article 13 — Speed and Distance Between Vehicles
1. Every driver of a vehicle shall in all circumstances have his vehicle
under control so as to be able to exercise due and proper care and to
react in time to any foreseeable event.
2. Every driver shall keep to a reasonable speed, and if he sees that
the speed limit prescribed by domestic legislation is about to be
exceeded or if this would constitute a danger to the traffic on the
road in question, he shall slow down and if necessary stop his vehicle.
3. The speed limit applicable on the road being used shall be observed.
4. At night and in conditions of poor visibility, drivers shall
adjust their speed to the limits of their headlight illumination.
5. Every driver of a vehicle following another vehicle shall keep at
a sufficient distance from that other vehicle to avoid a collision
if the vehicle in front should suddenly slow down or stop.

Article 14 — Priority
1. Every driver approaching an intersection shall exercise such care as
may be required by the local conditions.
2. A driver who is not on a priority road shall yield right of way to
vehicles travelling on the priority road.
3. Subject to the provisions of paragraph 2 of this Article, vehicles
shall at intersections yield right of way to vehicles approaching from
their right, unless domestic legislation establishes a different rule.
4. The principle of yielding right of way to vehicles from the right,
even if not a statutory rule, is widely applied in signatory states
and should be assumed by drivers unfamiliar with local rules.

Article 15 — Special Rules Applicable to Authorized Emergency Vehicles
Domestic legislation shall make provision for special rules regarding
the priority of duly authorized vehicles in service on urgent duty and
identifiable as such.

Article 16 — Changing Direction
1. Before turning right or left for the purpose of entering another road
or entering a property bordering on the road, every driver shall:
   (a) if he wishes to turn off on the side appropriate to the direction
       of traffic, keep as close as possible to that edge of the
       carriageway;
   (b) if he wishes to turn off on the other side, move as close as
       possible to the centre of the carriageway and, if he is
       leaving a road on which traffic moves in both directions to enter
       another such road, make the turn in such a way as to join the
       carriageway of the road he is entering on the appropriate side.
2. While changing direction, the driver shall yield right of way to
vehicles meeting him on the road he is about to enter and to cyclists
and moped riders moving along that road.

Article 17 — Slowing Down
Every driver intending to slow down shall, unless the slow-down is
imperceptible, first make sure that he can do so without risk or
inconvenience to other drivers. He shall give warning of his intention
unless he has good reason for not doing so.

Article 18 — Intersections and Obligation to Give Way
1. At every road intersection, a driver coming from a road giving
priority shall give way to vehicles coming from a priority road or to
vehicles which, by virtue of the regulation governing priority at that
intersection, have the right to cross first.
2. At intersections other than those governed by a give-way sign or by
traffic signals, drivers shall give way to vehicles coming from their
right.
3. At intersections between roads of different relative importance
determined by road signs and at intersections where rules of priority
are in force, every driver shall obey those rules.

Article 19 — Level Crossings
Road users shall exercise particular care at level crossings. In
particular:
(a) every driver of a vehicle shall approach a level crossing at a
    moderate speed and shall not cross the railway track unless he can
    see that no train or other rail vehicle is approaching;
(b) in particular, drivers shall not start to cross a level crossing
    when the crossing gate is closed or being closed, or when the red
    lights flash or the bell rings or rings repeatedly.

Article 20 — Rules Applicable to Pedestrians
1. If there are pavements or footpaths on a road, pedestrians shall
use them. Nevertheless, pedestrians pushing a cycle, a moped, or a
motorcycle, pedestrians pushing or carrying bulky objects, groups of
pedestrians being conducted by a person responsible for them, and
processions shall be permitted to use the carriageway.
2. If there are no pavements or footpaths, or if it is impracticable
to use them, pedestrians may walk on the carriageway; where there is
a cycle track and its use by pedestrians is not prohibited by
domestic legislation, pedestrians may use it, but shall not obstruct
cycle or moped traffic.
3. Pedestrians walking on the carriageway shall keep as close as possible
to the edge of the carriageway.
4. Pedestrians crossing a carriageway shall do so with caution; before
stepping on to the carriageway, they shall make sure that they can
do so without danger.
5. Pedestrians shall not cross a carriageway at an intersection where
traffic is controlled by traffic light signals, except when the traffic
light signal for pedestrians shows that they may cross.
6. Pedestrians shall cross the carriageway perpendicular to its axis,
either at pedestrian crossings, if any exist nearby, or at
intersections, or along a straight line if crossings and intersections
are too far away.

Article 21 — Behaviour of Drivers towards Pedestrians
1. Notwithstanding the provisions of Article 18, paragraph 1 of this
Convention, every driver shall, where necessary to ensure the safety of
pedestrians, yield right of way to pedestrians who have already stepped
on to or who are about to step on to a pedestrian crossing marked on
the carriageway by signs or road markings.
2. Drivers shall not endanger pedestrians by swerving or by driving
with undue speed near a pedestrian crossing.
3. Drivers shall not overtake another vehicle immediately before a
pedestrian crossing.

Article 22 — Rules Applicable to Cyclists
1. It is recommended that domestic legislation should provide that
cyclists shall not ride without holding the handlebars, shall not allow
themselves to be towed by another vehicle, and shall not carry, tow
or push loads or other persons in such a way as to endanger their
safety or to be a nuisance to other road users.
2. It is recommended that domestic legislation should also provide
that cyclists shall not ride side by side on the carriageway except
on paths or parts of the road reserved for cyclists.

Article 23 — Stopping and Parking
1. Outside built-up areas, vehicles, when stopped or parked, shall be
placed outside the carriageway if possible. Whether or not they are
placed on the carriageway, stopped or parked vehicles shall, if
possible, be placed on the side appropriate to the direction of
traffic.
2. Vehicles shall not be stopped or parked in a dangerous position or
in such circumstances as to create a danger or obstacle for traffic.
3. In particular, in built-up areas, if no parking is indicated by road
signs or road markings, every driver shall take care not to cause
inconvenience to the movement of other vehicles or pedestrians.

Article 24 — Opening of Doors
No person shall open the door of a vehicle, leave it open, or get out
of the vehicle without having made sure that he cannot thereby endanger
other road users.

Article 25 — Motorways and Similar Roads
1. On motorways and, if domestic legislation so provides, on specially
signposted fast-traffic roads:
   (a) the use of the road by pedestrians, animals, cycles, mopeds and
       all vehicles other than motor vehicles and their trailers, and
       by motor vehicles or trailers which cannot, by virtue of their
       design, exceed a set speed on a level road, shall be prohibited;
   (b) drivers shall not make U-turns, shall not drive in reverse, and
       shall not cross the central dividing strip, including gaps in it.
2. Drivers shall not stop or park on motorways except at specially
designated parking areas. If a vehicle is immobilized on the carriageway
or hard shoulder owing to a breakdown, the driver shall try to move it
off the carriageway as soon as possible and shall place the prescribed
warning device at an appropriate distance from the vehicle in order to
warn approaching traffic.
3. Drivers using a motorway shall join it only from the right (or left
in countries with left-hand traffic) by means of the approach roads.
They shall leave by the appropriate exit roads.

Article 26 — Special Rules Applicable to Convoys
Special rules may be laid down in domestic legislation for traffic
convoys. A convoy may not be overtaken or separated by other vehicles
when it is moving as a unit with its headlights on and announced by
auditory warning.

Article 27 — Special Rules Applicable to Cyclists, Moped Riders and Motorcyclists
1. It is recommended that domestic legislation should provide that
cyclists shall use only roads and sections of roads designated for them
or, failing this, as close to the edge of the carriageway as possible.
2. The provisions of paragraph 1 of this Article shall apply to moped
riders where domestic legislation so provides.
3. Motorcyclists and moped riders shall not hold on to another vehicle
in motion.

Article 28 — Auditory and Luminous Warnings
1. Auditory warning devices may be used only to give warning in order
to avoid an accident.
2. Drivers of motor vehicles shall use dipped headlights or, if the
motor vehicle is not equipped with dipped headlights, some other
appropriate light, when driving in built-up areas at night.
3. Drivers shall use dipped headlights when overtaking in built-up
areas at night and when visibility is adversely affected.
4. Outside built-up areas, drivers shall use main-beam headlights
in the absence of adequate road lighting. They shall switch to dipped
headlights when meeting oncoming vehicles and when following another
vehicle.

Article 29 — Rail-Borne Vehicles
On roads used by both rail-borne vehicles and other road users, the
latter shall give way to the rail-borne vehicles.

Article 30 — Loading of Vehicles
1. The load of a vehicle shall be so arranged and, if necessary, stowed
that:
   (a) it cannot endanger persons, damage public or private property,
       or cause nuisance (e.g., through noise, dust, smell);
   (b) it does not obstruct the driver's view or impair the stability
       or driving of the vehicle;
   (c) it does not obscure the lights or the distinguishing and
       registration marks of the vehicle.

Article 31 — Behaviour After an Accident
1. Every driver or other road user involved in a road accident shall
stop as soon as he can do so without causing a further danger to
traffic.
2. If a road accident results in injury, every driver or other road
user involved shall:
   (a) take or have taken all measures at his disposal to ensure the
       safety of traffic and, if a person is killed or seriously injured,
       to avoid, in so far as possible, any action that would make it
       more difficult to establish what happened;
   (b) inform the police or other competent authorities.
3. If a road accident involves only damage to property, every driver
or other road user involved shall take all measures at his disposal
to avoid danger to other road users.
4. Contracting Parties may, in their domestic legislation, require
persons involved in a road accident to give their name and address to
other persons involved.
5. If a road accident results in injury or death, Contracting Parties
shall require that the facts of the accident be reported to the police.

========================================================================
CHAPTER III — CONDITIONS FOR ADMISSION OF MOTOR VEHICLES AND TRAILERS
TO INTERNATIONAL TRAFFIC
========================================================================

Article 36 — Registration
1. In order to be admissible to international traffic, every motor
vehicle and every trailer coupled to a motor vehicle shall:
   (a) be registered by a Contracting Party and bear a registration
       number;
   (b) display at the rear, if it is a motor vehicle, its registration
       number, and if it is a trailer, its registration number or, where
       it is permitted by the legislation of the Contracting Party in
       whose territory the vehicle is registered, the registration number
       of the towing vehicle;
   (c) display at the rear the distinguishing sign of the State in
       whose territory it is registered.
2. A Contracting Party may refuse to admit to international traffic on
its territory any motor vehicle or trailer which does not comply with
the requirements of the present Convention.

Article 37 — Technical Conditions
1. Motor vehicles admitted to international traffic shall satisfy the
technical conditions laid down in Annex 5 to this Convention.
2. Trailers admitted to international traffic shall satisfy the technical
conditions laid down in Annex 5 to this Convention.

Article 38 — Lighting and Light-Signalling Devices
Motor vehicles shall be equipped with lighting and light-signalling
devices in accordance with the requirements of Annex 5.

Article 39 — Rear View Mirror and Windscreen Wipers
Every motor vehicle shall be equipped with at least one rear-view mirror
and, if it has a windscreen, with a windscreen wiper.

Article 41 — International Driving Permit
1. An international driving permit shall be issued in accordance with
the provisions of Annex 7 by a Contracting Party or by an association
duly authorized by that Contracting Party.
2. The international driving permit shall be valid for a maximum period
of three years from the date of issue or until the domestic driving
permit on which it is based expires, whichever is the shorter period.

========================================================================
CHAPTER IV — DRIVERS OF MOTOR VEHICLES
========================================================================

Article 41 — Driving Licences
1. Contracting Parties shall require every driver of a motor vehicle to
hold a driving licence. Each Contracting Party shall recognize:
   (a) any domestic driving permit issued in any other Contracting
       Party, or
   (b) any international driving permit,
   as authorizing the holder to drive in its territory vehicles of the
   category shown in those permits.
2. A domestic driving permit shall be recognized in the territory of
any Contracting Party until the document has expired or the holder
has established his normal residence in that territory.
3. Driving licences shall be issued only after a driving test; the
conditions of admission to the driving test shall be determined by
domestic legislation.
4. Every Contracting Party shall have the right not to recognize the
validity of driving permits issued to persons who, at the time they
were issued, had their normal residence in the territory of that
Contracting Party.

Article 42 — Suspension of Validity of Driving Permits
1. Contracting Parties may withdraw from a driver the right to use his
domestic or international driving permit in their territory, if he has
committed in their territory an offence disqualifying him from driving
under their domestic legislation.
2. Contracting Parties may withdraw from a driver the right to use his
domestic or international driving permit in their territory if he has
committed abroad an offence that would disqualify him from driving
under their domestic legislation.

========================================================================
ANNEX 5 — TECHNICAL CONDITIONS CONCERNING MOTOR VEHICLES AND TRAILERS
(Key provisions)
========================================================================

Section 1 — Braking
Every motor vehicle shall be equipped with a braking system which will
be effective on any gradient, in all loading conditions and at any speed
the motor vehicle can achieve, and which enables the driver to control
the motor vehicle safely and to reduce its speed or stop it in emergency
situations.

Section 2 — Lighting
Every motor vehicle shall be equipped with:
(a) two white or yellow front-position (side) lights;
(b) two red rear-position (tail) lights;
(c) two red stop-lights which can be seen by day and night;
(d) two white reversing lights (recommended);
(e) direction indicators at the front and rear;
(f) one white or yellow front fog lamp and one red rear fog lamp
    (required in bad weather).

Section 3 — Tyres
Tyres shall be of a type appropriate for the use to which the vehicle
is put, shall not be worn to the extent that the tread pattern is
indistinguishable, and shall comply with the minimum tread-depth
requirements set by domestic legislation (typically 1.6 mm across the
central three-quarters of the tread width and around the entire
circumference).

Section 4 — Speed Limiter
Heavy goods vehicles and buses admitted to international traffic are
recommended to be fitted with a speed limiter that prevents the vehicle
from exceeding the maximum speed prescribed for that category in the
Contracting Parties' domestic legislation.

========================================================================
ANNEX 6 — DOMESTIC DRIVING PERMIT (Key provisions)
========================================================================
The domestic driving permit shall show:
1. The distinguishing sign of the Contracting Party which issued it.
2. The title of the permit in the national language(s) of the
   Contracting Party.
3. The surname, first name(s), and date of birth of the holder.
4. The date of issue and the date of expiry of the permit.
5. The authority that issued the permit.
6. The categories of vehicles the holder is authorized to drive, in
   accordance with the categories defined in this Convention.

========================================================================
ANNEX 7 — INTERNATIONAL DRIVING PERMIT (Key provisions)
========================================================================
1. The International Driving Permit (IDP) is valid only when
   accompanied by the domestic driving permit to which it relates.
2. The IDP shall be printed in French, with translations in English,
   Russian, Spanish, Arabic, and Chinese.
3. The holder of the IDP is authorized to drive in all Contracting
   Parties the categories of vehicle for which the IDP has been issued,
   subject to any restrictions noted on the permit.
4. The IDP does not replace the need for local insurance or compliance
   with domestic traffic regulations of the host Contracting Party.

========================================================================
KEY PRINCIPLES APPLICABLE TO ALL SIGNATORY NATIONS
(Summary for DriveLegal users)
========================================================================

1. DRIVING SIDE: Each country chooses the side of the road (left or right),
   and this applies uniformly throughout its territory. As a visitor, you
   must observe the host country's rule.

2. SPEED: Obey posted speed limits. In the absence of a posted limit,
   general limits in built-up areas typically range from 30–50 km/h;
   outside built-up areas from 80–100 km/h; on motorways from 100–130 km/h.
   Exact limits differ by country.

3. ALCOHOL: Most signatory states set a blood-alcohol concentration (BAC)
   limit of 0.05 g/dl (50 mg/100 ml) or lower. Some apply 0.00 g/dl to new
   or professional drivers. Violation is a serious offence in all signatories.

4. PRIORITY TO THE RIGHT: At unmarked intersections, give way to vehicles
   approaching from the right (in right-hand traffic countries). This rule
   applies unless overridden by a road sign.

5. PEDESTRIAN CROSSINGS: Drivers must give way to pedestrians at marked
   pedestrian crossings, even where no traffic light is present.

6. OVERTAKING: Overtake on the side away from oncoming traffic. Do not
   overtake at or near intersections, level crossings, hilltops, or bends.

7. HEADLIGHTS: Use dipped headlights in poor visibility, at night in
   built-up areas, and when meeting oncoming vehicles.

8. ACCIDENTS: Stop immediately. Assist injured persons. Report to police
   if injury or death occurred.

9. DRIVING LICENCE: Foreign visitors driving on a domestic licence must
   carry that licence. An International Driving Permit (IDP) is required
   in many signatory states when using a domestic licence issued in a
   non-signatory country or a licence not in Latin script.

10. VEHICLE CONDITION: The vehicle must meet roadworthiness standards of
    the host country (lighting, brakes, tyres, reflectors) and display
    valid registration marks.
