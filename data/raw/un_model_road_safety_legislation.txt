UN MODEL LEGISLATION ON ROAD SAFETY
Prepared by the United Nations Secretary-General pursuant to
General Assembly resolution 74/299 (2020) — Road Safety 2021–2030
Based on the Global Plan for the Decade of Action for Road Safety 2021–2030
[Adapted for legal-awareness purposes. Source: UN Road Safety Trust Fund / UNECE]

========================================================================
PREAMBLE
========================================================================
This model legislation is intended to assist Member States and Contracting
Parties to the Vienna Convention on Road Traffic (1968) in enacting or
updating their domestic road safety laws. The provisions below represent
the international consensus on minimum standards derived from evidence-based
best practices and the WHO Global Status Report on Road Safety.

Member States are encouraged to adopt these provisions or provisions
that provide at least equivalent protection as part of their national
legislation.

========================================================================
PART I — SPEED
========================================================================

Section 1 — General Speed Standards
1.1 Speed limits shall be set based on safe system principles, taking
    into account road design, traffic composition, and the consequence of
    crashes for all road users, including pedestrians and cyclists.

1.2 Default speed limits recommended for adoption:
    (a) Built-up area (urban zone):     ≤ 50 km/h; 30 km/h near schools,
        hospitals, and residential zones;
    (b) Rural road (single carriageway): ≤ 80–90 km/h;
    (c) Dual carriageway (non-motorway): ≤ 100 km/h;
    (d) Motorway / expressway:           ≤ 120–130 km/h.

1.3 Member States should empower local authorities to set lower limits on
    specific roads, including 20–30 km/h zones in areas with significant
    pedestrian activity.

1.4 Automated speed enforcement (speed cameras, average-speed systems) is
    recognized as a highly effective countermeasure. The model legislation
    recommends that evidence from automated enforcement be admissible as
    prima facie evidence of a speed violation.

Section 2 — Penalties for Speeding
2.1 Penalties for speeding should be proportionate to the excess speed and
    should include, as a minimum:
    (a) Fixed or variable monetary fine;
    (b) Licence penalty points for the holder;
    (c) Licence suspension for serious excess (recommended: >30 km/h
        above the limit or recidivist offending);
    (d) Vehicle impoundment at the discretion of the authority.

2.2 Repeat offenders (three or more speed offences within 24 months) should
    face mandatory licence suspension of not less than 30 days.

========================================================================
PART II — ALCOHOL AND DRUG-IMPAIRED DRIVING
========================================================================

Section 3 — Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) Limits
3.1 General drivers: maximum BAC of 0.05 g/dl (50 mg/100 ml of blood).
3.2 Novice and professional drivers: maximum BAC of 0.02 g/dl or zero
    tolerance in accordance with domestic law.
3.3 Motorcycle riders and commercial vehicle operators: same stricter limit
    applicable to novice drivers.
3.4 Random breath testing (RBT) by authorized police officers is recommended
    as mandatory authority. No reasonable suspicion need be established before
    breath-testing a driver.

Section 4 — Drug-Impaired Driving
4.1 Driving under the influence of psychoactive substances (including cannabis,
    opioids, benzodiazepines, amphetamines, and cocaine) that impair driving
    ability is prohibited.
4.2 Member States should establish per se limits for controlled substances
    in blood, saliva, or urine, and authorize roadside testing.

Section 5 — Penalties for Impaired Driving
5.1 First offence (BAC 0.05–0.08 g/dl): Fixed fine + minimum 90-day
    licence suspension.
5.2 First offence (BAC > 0.08 g/dl): Heavy fine + minimum 6-month
    licence suspension + vehicle impoundment.
5.3 Repeat offence: Criminal prosecution + mandatory imprisonment not
    less than 48 hours + licence disqualification.
5.4 Causing death or grievous harm while impaired: Enhanced penalty with
    mandatory custodial sentence.
5.5 Alcohol interlock devices for reinstatement of licence after
    disqualification are recommended for high-risk offenders.

========================================================================
PART III — SEAT BELTS AND CHILD RESTRAINTS
========================================================================

Section 6 — Seat Belt Use
6.1 All occupants of a motor vehicle equipped with seat belts shall
    wear the seat belt at all times while the vehicle is in motion,
    unless exempted under domestic legislation (e.g., medical waiver).
6.2 The driver is responsible for ensuring that all passengers under 16
    years of age wear appropriate restraints.
6.3 Exceptions may be granted for vehicles used for reversed transport
    of passengers, emergency medical personnel while attending a patient,
    and similarly prescribed situations.
6.4 Penalty: Fixed fine; repeat offence may attract licence penalty points.

Section 7 — Child Restraint Systems
7.1 Children below the height of 135 cm (or the minimum height determined
    by domestic legislation) shall be seated in an approved child restraint
    system (forward-facing seat, rear-facing seat, or booster seat as
    appropriate for age and weight).
7.2 Child restraint systems shall comply with UNECE Regulation No. 44 or
    Regulation No. 129 (i-Size) or equivalent national standard.
7.3 Children shall not travel in the front seat of a vehicle unless the
    air-bag on that side is deactivated and the appropriate child seat is
    fitted.
7.4 Penalty: Fixed fine payable by the driver.

========================================================================
PART IV — MOTORCYCLE HELMETS
========================================================================

Section 8 — Helmet Requirements
8.1 Every rider and pillion passenger on a powered two-wheeled or
    three-wheeled vehicle shall wear a protective helmet at all times
    while the vehicle is in motion.
8.2 Helmets shall comply with standards equivalent to UNECE Regulation
    No. 22 or equivalent national/regional standard.
8.3 Fastening straps shall be secured at all times.
8.4 Penalty: Fixed fine; domestic legislation may also provide for
    licence penalty points or vehicle impoundment.
8.5 Member States are encouraged to apply the same requirement to
    electric two-wheelers capable of speeds exceeding 25 km/h.

========================================================================
PART V — MOBILE PHONE / DISTRACTED DRIVING
========================================================================

Section 9 — Handheld Device Prohibition
9.1 Drivers shall not hold or use a handheld mobile phone or any handheld
    electronic communication device while driving.
9.2 Use of a hands-free device is permitted provided it does not require
    the driver to take both hands off the wheel and does not impair driving.
9.3 The prohibition applies while the vehicle is stationary at a traffic
    light or in slow-moving congestion.
9.4 Penalty: Fixed fine + mandatory licence penalty points; repeat
    offence within 24 months: licence suspension of at least 30 days.

Section 10 — Broader Distracted Driving
10.1 Domestic legislation should prohibit driving while significantly
     distracted by any activity, including eating, reading, or operating
     in-vehicle entertainment systems.

========================================================================
PART VI — INFRASTRUCTURE AND VEHICLE SAFETY
========================================================================

Section 11 — Road Infrastructure Safety
11.1 Member States should adopt road infrastructure safety inspection and
     assessment frameworks, based on the UNECE Convention on Road Traffic
     and WHO recommendations.
11.2 All new or substantially reconstructed roads in the national network
     should undergo a Road Safety Audit conducted by a certified independent
     expert before opening to traffic.
11.3 High-risk road sections (black spots) should be identified and remediated
     on a priority basis with allocated budgets.

Section 12 — Vehicle Safety Standards
12.1 Member States should require that new motor vehicles comply with a
     minimum set of safety technologies, including:
     (a) Electronic stability control (ESC) for all passenger cars;
     (b) Anti-lock braking system (ABS) for all powered two-wheelers above
         150 cc;
     (c) Seat belt reminders for all front and rear seats;
     (d) Pedestrian detection / automatic emergency braking (AEB) for
         all new passenger cars (target: mandatory by 2025–2030).
12.2 Type-approval based on UNECE vehicle regulations is recommended as
     the baseline technical standard.

========================================================================
PART VII — ENFORCEMENT AND CROSS-BORDER MATTERS
========================================================================

Section 13 — General Enforcement Principles
13.1 Traffic enforcement authorities shall deploy evidence-based strategies
     targeting the fatal five: speeding, drink-driving, non-use of seat
     belts, non-use of helmets, and distracted driving.
13.2 Fines and penalties collected shall be ring-fenced, to the extent
     possible, for road safety improvement programmes.
13.3 Administrative licence suspension (immediate, before judicial review)
     is recommended for serious offences such as high BAC, very high speed,
     and abandonment of scene after a fatal accident.

Section 14 — Cross-Border Enforcement
14.1 Member States are encouraged to enter into bilateral or multilateral
     agreements providing for mutual recognition and enforcement of road
     traffic offence decisions issued in another signatory state.
14.2 Where a foreign-registered vehicle's owner is identified through
     vehicle registration data exchange (comparable to EU Directive 2015/413),
     the offence notice may be sent to the registered address in the owner's
     home country.
14.3 Member States should establish national contact points for vehicle
     registration data exchange to facilitate cross-border enforcement.

========================================================================
PART VIII — POST-CRASH RESPONSE
========================================================================

Section 15 — Emergency Response
15.1 Member States shall maintain adequate pre-hospital trauma care systems
     capable of reaching any point on the road network within defined
     response-time targets.
15.2 Drivers involved in a collision shall stop, render first aid or summon
     assistance, and notify law enforcement where injury or fatality occurred.
15.3 Obstruction of the post-crash scene or failure to stop after an injury
     collision shall be a criminal offence.

Section 16 — Data Collection
16.1 Member States should maintain a national road crash database that records
     crash location, severity, contributing factors, and vehicle and road
     characteristics, and that is compatible with WHO Minimum Data Set
     standards.

========================================================================
KEY PRINCIPLES APPLICABLE TO ALL UN MEMBER STATES
(Summary for DriveLegal users)
========================================================================

1. SPEED LIMITS: Obey posted limits. Default urban limit is 50 km/h (or lower
   near schools/hospitals). Exceeding limits by 30+ km/h typically triggers
   licence suspension in most jurisdictions.

2. BLOOD ALCOHOL: Do not drive if you have consumed alcohol above 0.05 g/dl.
   Many countries apply stricter limits (0.02 or zero) for new and professional
   drivers and motorcyclists. Random breath testing is legal in most countries.

3. SEAT BELTS: Mandatory for all occupants in all seats wherever fitted.
   The driver is responsible for passengers under 16.

4. HELMETS: Mandatory for all riders and pillion passengers on powered
   two-wheelers. Helmet must be properly fastened.

5. MOBILE PHONES: Do not hold or use a mobile phone while driving, including
   at traffic lights. Use hands-free only.

6. CHILD SEATS: Children under 135 cm tall must use an approved child
   restraint. Do not place a rear-facing child seat in the front where an
   active airbag is fitted.

7. ACCIDENTS: You must stop, assist, and report to police if anyone is injured.
   Fleeing the scene is a criminal offence everywhere.

8. VEHICLE CONDITION: Tyres, brakes, lights, and registration must be
   compliant with the host country's standards at all times.
